Pretrained foundation models for vision tasks reprezintă modele vizuale antrenate pe volume foarte mari de date, capabile să învețe reprezentări generale transferabile către multiple sarcini precum clasificare, detecție și segmentare de imagini.

CLIP
Contrastive Language–Image Pretraining (CLIP) is a 2021 research paper by OpenAI that introduced a large-scale multimodal model linking text and images. It demonstrated that natural-language supervision—rather than task-specific datasets—could yield robust image recognition capabilities across many tasks without additional training.

Key facts
Full title: Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision

Authors: Alec Radford et al. (OpenAI, 2021)

Model type: Multimodal contrastive learning

Training data: 400 million image–text pairs scraped from the internet

Core output: Joint embedding space for text and images

Approach and architecture
CLIP trains two neural networks—one for text, one for images—so that matching image–caption pairs produce similar embeddings. The method uses a contrastive loss to align these modalities within a shared latent space. This setup enables zero-shot transfer: given text labels, the model can classify previously unseen images without fine-tuning.

Key contributions
The paper showed that internet-scale image–text data could replace manual labeling for many vision benchmarks. CLIP’s performance approached or exceeded supervised models across diverse datasets while requiring no task-specific retraining. It also provided a general foundation for downstream multimodal tasks.

Influence and applications
CLIP’s release catalyzed research in multimodal learning and inspired generative systems such as DALL·E and Stable Diffusion. Its text–image embedding technique underpins prompt-based control in diffusion models, retrieval systems, and vision–language reasoning benchmarks.
