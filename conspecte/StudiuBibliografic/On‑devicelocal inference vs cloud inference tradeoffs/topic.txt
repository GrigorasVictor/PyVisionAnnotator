On-device inference permite executarea modelelor AI direct pe dispozitivul utilizatorului, oferind latență redusă și protecția datelor, în timp ce cloud inference utilizează resurse computaționale externe pentru performanță și scalabilitate superioară.


Neurosurgeon: Collaborative Intelligence Between the Cloud and Mobile Edge is a 2017 research paper proposing a framework for splitting deep neural network computations between cloud servers and mobile devices. The work addresses the challenge of enabling resource-limited devices to run deep learning models efficiently without offloading all computation to the cloud.

Key facts
Authors: Yuanchao Shu, Jingzhao Sun, et al. (AUTORI NU SUNT BUNI)

Published in: Proceedings of the 22nd International Conference on Architectural Support for Programming Languages and Operating Systems (ASPLOS 2017)

Core concept: Split deep neural networks across cloud and edge devices

Goal: Improve latency, energy efficiency, and privacy for mobile AI applications

Background
Traditional deep learning relies on cloud computation due to the large resource requirements of neural networks. However, constant communication with cloud servers introduces latency and privacy concerns. Neurosurgeon explores how to partition network layers intelligently so that part of the computation runs locally on a mobile device while the rest executes in the cloud.

Methodology
The paper introduces a decision engine that dynamically determines where to divide a neural network between the edge and the cloud. It considers real-time metrics such as bandwidth, energy consumption, and inference accuracy. The partitioning strategy allows for adaptive deployment of deep learning models depending on the available resources and network conditions.

Impact and Applications
Neurosurgeon helped pioneer the concept of collaborative intelligence, influencing later research in edge AI and federated learning. Its approach demonstrated substantial reductions in inference latency and data transfer while preserving accuracy, making it an early and influential step toward efficient on-device intelligence.

Legacy
Following its publication, the Neurosurgeon framework became a reference point for hybrid edge–cloud AI systems. It has inspired ongoing work in distributed neural network execution and edge computing optimization within both academic and industrial AI research communities.
