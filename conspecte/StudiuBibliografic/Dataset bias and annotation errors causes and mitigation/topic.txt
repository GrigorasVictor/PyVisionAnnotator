A Survey on Bias and Fairness in Machine Learning
A Survey on Bias and Fairness in Machine Learning is a scholarly review paper that systematically examines how bias arises in machine learning systems and how fairness can be measured and mitigated. It is frequently cited as a foundational reference for researchers and practitioners working on algorithmic fairness, ethical AI, and responsible data science.

Key facts
Focus: Comprehensive overview of bias sources and fairness interventions in ML

Themes: Data bias, algorithmic bias, fairness metrics, mitigation strategies

Typical scope: Literature up to late 2010s

Usage: Core reference in AI ethics and fairness research

Background and Scope
The paper surveys the landscape of bias and fairness research in machine learning, highlighting how social, data-driven, and algorithmic processes can propagate inequities. It reviews both theoretical and empirical studies across domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, and decision-making systems. The goal is to create a conceptual framework that connects fairness definitions, bias typologies, and methodological interventions.

Sources of Bias
Bias is analyzed as arising from multiple levels: sampling bias in datasets, measurement bias in features and labels, and algorithmic bias introduced by model architectures or optimization goals. The survey emphasizes that bias is not solely a technical problem but reflects broader sociotechnical contexts, including historical inequalities and institutional practices.

Fairness Metrics and Mitigation

A major contribution of the paper is its taxonomy of fairness definitions—such as demographic parity, equalized odds, and predictive parity—and the trade-offs between them. It discusses mitigation techniques at three stages of the ML pipeline: pre-processing (e.g., reweighting data), in-processing (e.g., fairness constraints or adversarial debiasing), and post-processing (e.g., output adjustment).

Impact and Ongoing Developments
The survey has influenced both academic and policy discussions about ethical AI. It has guided the creation of fairness toolkits and informed standards for auditing machine learning models. Subsequent research has built on its taxonomy to address fairness under distribution shift, intersectional bias, and causal perspectives on discrimination.
