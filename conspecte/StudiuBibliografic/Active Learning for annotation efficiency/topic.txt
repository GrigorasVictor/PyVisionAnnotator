Active learning (AL) is one of the most important approaches for reducing annotation cost in machine learning pipelines. Instead of labeling data randomly, active learning systems iteratively select the most informative samples for annotation, thereby minimizing human effort while maximizing model performance.

Modern annotation platforms increasingly integrate:

uncertainty sampling,
human-in-the-loop learning,
semi-automatic labeling,
and model-assisted annotation workflows.

These techniques are particularly important because large-scale datasets for computer vision are expensive and time-consuming to annotate manually.





Active Learning Literature Survey
The Active Learning Literature Survey is a 2009 technical report authored by Burr Settles at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. It provides one of the most widely cited overviews of active learning—a subfield of machine learning focused on reducing labeling costs by selectively choosing data points to annotate.

Key facts
Author: Burr Settles

Institution: University of Wisconsin–Madison

Publication year: 2009

Type: Technical report (Computer Sciences TR1648)

Focus: Survey of methods and theory in active learning

Context and purpose
At the time of publication, active learning had emerged as a promising solution to the challenge of obtaining large amounts of labeled data for supervised learning. Settles’ report synthesized scattered literature across statistics, pattern recognition, and natural language processing into a coherent taxonomy and reference guide for researchers and practitioners.

Content overview
The report categorizes active learning strategies into several major frameworks—such as membership query synthesis, stream-based selective sampling, and pool-based sampling—each describing how an algorithm queries unlabeled instances. It also reviews query selection heuristics (e.g., uncertainty sampling, query-by-committee, expected model change) and their theoretical motivations.
